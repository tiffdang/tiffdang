# AI learning journey: content testing plan

## Positioning

**An experienced product marketer learning AI by building real research, positioning, and decision systems in public.**

The content should demonstrate curiosity and judgment—not perform expertise or present AI output as finished truth.

## Channel roles

| Channel | Purpose | Best format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TikTok | Test hooks and accessible explanations | 20–60 second video |
| Instagram Reels | Test storytelling and visual identity | 30–90 second video |
| GitHub | Preserve evidence, methods, and iteration | Research and working artifacts |
| Portfolio | Present polished thinking and outcomes | Case studies |
| LinkedIn | Translate proven ideas into professional insight | Short post, carousel, or article |

## The publishing loop

1. Build or research something real.
2. Extract one surprising, useful lesson.
3. Test two or three hooks on TikTok or Instagram.
4. Track retention, saves, shares, comments, and profile visits.
5. Improve the idea using audience questions and objections.
6. Add the deeper artifact to GitHub or the portfolio.
7. Publish a professional synthesis on LinkedIn.

## Starter series

### 1. Building a PMM case study in public

**Hook:** “I’m applying for a product marketing role in biotech, so I analyzed what the company actually needs—not just the job description.”

**Payoff:** Explain the shift from ELN/LIMS perception to AI-ready R&D infrastructure.

### 2. AI needs context, not just a model

**Hook:** “The biggest problem with AI in biotech may not be the model. It may be the spreadsheet on someone’s desktop.”

**Payoff:** Show why structured experimental data, lineage, and workflow context matter.

### 3. How I research a company before applying

**Hook:** “Most candidates read the About page. I map the product, buyers, competitors, business model, and strategic tension.”

**Payoff:** Walk through the research framework and show one Benchling example.

### 4. What AI got wrong

**Hook:** “AI gave me a confident market-share number. I refused to use it.”

**Payoff:** Explain evidence quality, source hierarchy, and why uncertainty increases credibility.

### 5. Feature versus platform

**Hook:** “Adding an AI assistant does not make a product an AI platform.”

**Payoff:** Offer a simple test: context, workflow, action, governance, and compounding data.

### 6. Learning biotech without pretending to be a scientist

**Hook:** “You do not need a biology degree to market biotech software—but you do need to respect the workflow.”

**Payoff:** Share the vocabulary and workflow map being learned for the case study.

## Guardrails

- Label speculative portfolio work clearly.
- Never imply access to confidential company or customer information.
- Separate company-reported metrics from independently verified facts.
- Do not present AI-generated research without checking sources.
- Lead with a useful idea, not a job-search announcement.
- Avoid turning every post into a pitch for employment.

## Initial test metrics

For the first 12 videos, optimize for learning rather than follower count:

- Three-second hold rate
- Average watch time and completion rate
- Saves and shares per view
- Substantive comments or questions
- Profile visits
- Which problem, audience, and presentation style earn repeated interest

After 12 posts, select the two strongest themes for deeper portfolio and LinkedIn content.
